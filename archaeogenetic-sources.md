# Archaeogenetic sources — Europa Strata

Short working bibliography for the interactive map. Prefer post-2015 genome-wide studies and recent syntheses. Percentages in the UI are **approximate consensus ranges**; models differ by qpAdm setup, reference panels, and regional sampling. Archaeological culture names are not genetic clusters.

The atlas covers **Mesolithic → present**, including Nordic Bronze Age, Wielbark, Frankish/Carolingian, and later empires. For historical polities after ~500 BCE, genetics usually means **continuity of the WHG–EEF–steppe ternary** plus documented medieval/imperial admixture — not invented empire-specific components.

## Genetic component glossary (UI)

- **WHG** — Western Hunter-Gatherers  
- **EHG** — Eastern Hunter-Gatherers (ANE-rich forest-steppe)  
- **SHG** — Scandinavian Hunter-Gatherers (WHG + elevated EHG-related affinity in the north)  
- **EEF** — Early European Farmers (Anatolian Neolithic–related)  
- **WSH** — Western Steppe Herders (Yamnaya-related; UI “Steppe”)  
- **CHG** — Caucasus Hunter-Gatherers (usually inside the steppe cline)

## Foundational turnovers (Europe)

- **Haak et al. (2015)** — *Nature*. Massive migration from the steppe was a source for Indo-European languages in Europe. Yamnaya-related ancestry into Middle Neolithic Europe; Corded Ware as heavily steppe-admixed.
- **Allentoft et al. (2015)** — *Nature* (“Population genomics of Bronze Age Eurasia”). Bronze Age migrations reshape Europe/Asia.
- **Lazaridis et al. (2014–2016)** — *Nature* / related. Three major ancestral strands: WHG, EEF / Anatolian Neolithic-related, and steppe (via EHG–CHG mixtures).
- **Mathieson et al. (2015, 2018)** — *Nature*. Selection and Neolithic–Bronze Age structure; farmer–forager gradients.

## Bell Beaker, Britain, Iberia

- **Olalde et al. (2018)** — *Nature*. In Britain, Beaker-associated newcomers replace ~90% of the Neolithic gene pool; continental Beaker genetics are heterogeneous (Iberian Beakers ≠ steppe-rich central Beakers).
- **Olalde et al. (2019)** — *Science*. Iberian Peninsula: more gradual, often male-biased steppe-related influx than Britain’s near-replacement.

## Corded Ware, Denmark, France, Pontic steppe

- **Allentoft et al. (2024)** — *Nature* 625:301–311 (“Population genomics of post-glacial western Eurasia”). Black Sea–Baltic “great divide”; Yamnaya-related ancestry ~5,000 bp; Middle Don HG → Yamnaya; Globular Amphora admixture before Corded Ware expansion.
- **Allentoft / Willerslev (2024)** — *Nature* (Denmark 100 genomes). Second near-complete turnover: Single Grave with **~60–85%** steppe-related ancestry.
- **Science Advances (2024)** — Paris Basin Late Neolithic: real-time steppe ancestry in France; pulses ~3000/2900 and ~2600 BCE.
- **Science Advances (2025)** — CW/BB burial rites vs steppe ancestry not strictly synchronized (culture ≠ genes).
- **Nature (2024)** — “A genomic history of the North Pontic Region…” (s41586-024-08372-2). Usatove, Serednii Stih, Yamna formation; Mykhailivka epicentre.

## Nordic Bronze Age & later prehistoric facies

- Nordic Bronze Age peoples sit on **post–Single Grave / Battle Axe** ancestry; the cultural fluorescence (amber, bronzes, rock art) is not a third Denmark-scale genetic replacement. Use Late Neolithic/Bronze Age Scandinavian papers in the Allentoft 2024 ecosystem and earlier Battle Axe studies (e.g. Malmström et al. and related).
- Tumulus / Urnfield / Atlantic Bronze / Terramare: treated in-map as **cultural facies** on already-established WHG–EEF–steppe mixes unless a cited paper shows a local pulse.

## Iron Age → Classical → Imperial

- **Antonio et al. (2019)** — *Science*. Ancient Rome: Iron Age central Italians already mixed; Imperial Rome elevated eastern Mediterranean ancestry; medieval samples rebalance.
- Hallstatt / La Tène / “Celtic”: linguistic-cultural labels on BA-derived ancestry — not a separate deep component.
- Scythian/Sarmatian: variable west–east; many groups show East Eurasian admixture on steppe bases (multiple 2017–2021 steppe nomad aDNA papers).

## Migration Period, Wielbark, early medieval

- **Wielbark culture** (Pomerania/Poland, c. 1st–5th c. CE): archaeological complex linked to early Gothic traditions. Genome-wide sampling is thinner than Neolithic corpora; the map shows it as a **named cultural horizon** with plausible east-central European IA–early medieval ancestry (BA ternary + mobility), not a unique genetic cluster.
- Anglo-Saxon England: multiple early medieval Britain papers (e.g. Gretzinger et al. 2022 *Nature* and related) support substantial continental northern European influx in the east with regional Romano-British continuity — **admixture**, not blank-slate replacement everywhere.
- Visigothic / Frankish / Ostrogothic kingdoms: elite-layered rule over Roman provincial majorities — **absorption** pattern demographically.

## Frankish Empire & High/Late Medieval

- Carolingian political unification does **not** invent a new ancestry component; western European deep structure remains Neolithic–Bronze Age ternary with regional early medieval tweaks.
- Slavic medieval expansions in Central/Eastern Europe and the Balkans: supported by emerging medieval aDNA (regional papers 2020s); proportions vary sharply by site — UI uses elevated “other” / uncertainty rather than false precision.
- Al-Andalus: North African / Near Eastern–related gene flow in some medieval Muslim Iberian contexts; Christian north more continuous (Olalde Iberia follow-ups and medieval Iberia aDNA).

## Early modern → present

- Empires (Habsburg, Ottoman, PLC, Russia, Atlantic empires) redraw sovereignty and move people; they rarely reset the continental ternary outside specific corridors (ports, Ottoman Balkans, imperial capitals).
- Present-day fine-scale structure (e.g. People of the British Isles; European POPRES/related) often echoes early medieval and deeper landscapes more than 19th-century borders.
- Modern global migration diversifies cities — flagged in UI as rising “other,” not Paleo-components.

## Map extents

The live site paints **schematic culture/polity extents** onto a Natural Earth country basemap (public domain). Overlaps use roles (`incoming` / `dominant` / `substrate` / `fringe`) to show expansion and absorption. These are consensus best-guesses aligned with standard distribution maps (e.g. Corded Ware Rhine–Volga, Yamnaya Pontic–Caspian, Bell Beaker west/central, Roman provinces, Carolingian core, Ottoman Rumelia) — **not** excavation-point GIS layers. Modern borders are a cartographic convenience.

## Linguistic layer (languages ≠ genes)

The map pairs each region/period with a **language family / situation**, named languages when known, and an evidence tag:

- **attested** — written or epigraphic (Linear B Greek, Gaulish, Latin, Old Norse, etc.)
- **reconstructed** — comparative linguistics (PIE, pre-Germanic, early Celtic pathways)
- **hypothetical** — prehistory without attestation (Mesolithic/Neolithic speech)
- **mixed** — attested pieces plus uncertain layers (e.g. Wielbark↔Gothic narrative)

Indo-European dispersal is often **correlated** with steppe-related ancestry in temperate Europe (Haak 2015 and successors), but Iberian Beaker genetics and Science Advances work on CW/BB rites show culture and genes can decouple — speech can too. Basque and historical Iberian remain important non-IE survivals; Etruscan is non-IE in Italy; Finnish is Uralic beside North Germanic Scandinavia.

Standard references for the linguistic side (not aDNA): IE comparative handbooks; Mallory & Adams; Anthony (*The Horse, the Wheel, and Language*) as a synthesis bridging archaeology and IE; regional historical linguistics for Romance/Germanic/Slavic/Celtic.

## Method notes (UI honesty)

1. **Culture ≠ genome ≠ language.** Beaker, Corded Ware, Wielbark, Frankish Empire, Ottoman Europe are archaeological or political packages.
2. **Replacement vs absorption.** Britain Beaker (~90%) and Denmark farmer→steppe turnovers vs Iberia gradual steppe vs Frankish/Visigothic elite absorption — language shift can follow elite dominance without genetic replacement (and vice versa).
3. **Percentages.** Values such as “~90%,” “~60–85% steppe” are rounded literature ranges.
4. **Later history.** After the Bronze Age, slider stops prioritize **named polities** with continuity/admixture narrative; ancestry charts stay schematic; languages are often better attested than genomes for the same centuries.

1. Lazaridis / Haak / Allentoft 2015
